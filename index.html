The hook

This LED looks dim. Is it getting less electricity?

Slide time speed to the right to slow down time.

At normal speed it looks like a dim, steady light.

Slow time down and look at what it's really doing.

It isn't half-on. It's all the way on, then all the way off, hundreds of times a second. Your eyes can't keep up, so your brain averages it into "dim."

Don't take my word for it. You're going to build it and prove it yourself.

Step 1
Worksheet · Checkpoint 1

Human PWM: can you make it look dim?

Press the button. The LED is fully on. Let go: fully off.

Your challenge: tap the button as fast as you possibly can and try to make the LED look like a steady, half-bright light.

Get a neighbor to count your taps for 5 seconds.

What's going on

You can only make on or off with a button. There's no "half."

Making it look half-bright means switching faster than your eye can follow. How fast is that? Step 2 finds out.

The Arduino can switch a pin thousands of times faster than your thumb.
Step 2 · Slow motion
Worksheet · Checkpoint 2

Hand the switching to the Arduino

Rewire: remove the jumper from row 22 → row 10. Add a jumper from Arduino pin 9 → row 10.

int onTime  = 500;   // milliseconds ON
int offTime = 500;   // milliseconds OFF

void setup() {
  pinMode(9, OUTPUT);
}

void loop() {
  digitalWrite(9, HIGH);
  delay(onTime);
  digitalWrite(9, LOW);
  delay(offTime);
}

The experiment

  1. Upload. You'll see a slow blink (old news).
  2. Cut both numbers in half: 500 → 250 → 125 → 62 → 31 → 15 → 8 → 4 → 2.
  3. After every upload, write in your table: blink, flicker, or steady.
  4. Find the row where the blinking disappears for you.
Flashes per second = 1000 ÷ (onTime + offTime)
500 + 500 = 1000 → 1 flash per second

Why the blink vanished

Your brain is averaging

Past a certain speed, your eye and brain stop seeing separate flashes. They blend them into one steady light.

This is called flicker fusion. For most people it happens somewhere around 50–90 flashes per second.

The LED never got dimmer and never got "less electricity." It just started switching faster than you can see.

You've been fooled before

  • Movies: 24 still pictures per second look like motion.
  • LED car tail lights: move your eyes quickly past one at night and you'll see a dotted trail.
  • Phone screens: many dim themselves by flickering.
Try it: look at your LED out of the corner of your eye at the row where it just turned steady. Peripheral vision catches flicker better.
Step 3 · Duty cycle

On your Arduino: try these in your code and rank them by brightness:

9/1 · 7/3 · 5/5 · 3/7 · 1/9

Name it

PWM = Pulse Width Modulation

Duty cycle

duty = on ÷ (on + off)

7 ms on, 3 ms off → 7 ÷ 10 = 70%

Average voltage

average = duty × 5 V

70% × 5 V = 3.5 V

  • Pulse: one "on" burst
  • Width: how long that burst lasts
  • Modulation: changing it on purpose
A digital pin can only make 0 V or 5 V. PWM lets it act like any voltage in between by changing how much of the time it's on.

Step 5 · The knob

  
Worksheet · Checkpoint 5

  
Meet the potentiometer

  

    

      
A potentiometer ("pot") is a resistor with a slider inside. Turning the knob moves the slider, called the wiper.

      
    
        
  • Outer leg → + rail (5 V)
    • 
        
  • Other outer leg → − rail (GND)
    • 
        
  • Middle leg (wiper) → Arduino A0
    • 
      

      
Each leg goes in its own row.

      
Which pot do you have? Look for a printed number:
103 = 10,000 Ω (10K)  ·  104 = 100,000 Ω (100K)
Either one works today. Write yours on the worksheet.

Turn the knob, follow the numbers

void setup() {
  pinMode(9, OUTPUT);
  Serial.begin(9600);
}

void loop() {
  int potValue = analogRead(A0);  // 0 to 1023
  int brightness = potValue / 4;  // 0 to 255
  analogWrite(9, brightness);

  Serial.print("pot: ");
  Serial.print(potValue);
  Serial.print("   brightness: ");
  Serial.println(brightness);
  delay(50);
}

Someone always asks

  
"Why not just use the pot to dim the LED, or slow a motor?"

  

    

      
Pot in the path of the current

      
    
        
  • Your 10K pot lets through at most 0.5 mA. A TT motor wants hundreds of mA. It wouldn't even twitch.
    • 
        
  • A pot big enough to carry motor current turns the extra energy into heat.
    • 
        
  • Your robot's code can't turn a knob.
    • 
      

    

    

      
Pot as an input, PWM does the work

      
    
        
  • The pot only carries a tiny signal to A0.
    • 
        
  • The Arduino decides the duty cycle. It could come from a knob, a sensor, or an if statement.
    • 
        
  • An obstacle-avoiding robot can slow itself down as a wall gets close. No hands needed.
    • 
      

    

  

  
The knob tells the Arduino what it wants. PWM does it.

Wrap-up

  
Case closed.

  

    

      
    
        
  • A digital pin is only ever 0 V or 5 V.
    • 
        
  • PWM switches it fast enough to fool your eyes (and motors).
    • 
        
  • Duty cycle = how much of the time it's on.
    • 
        
  • analogWrite(pin, 0–255) sets the duty cycle on ~ pins.
    • 
        
  • analogRead(A0) gives 0–1023. Divide by 4 to fit.
